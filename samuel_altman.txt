Samuel Harris Altman (born April 22, 1985) is an American entrepreneur and investor who has served as the CEO of OpenAI since 2019, except for a brief period in November 2023 when he was ousted and then reinstated. He is considered one of the key figures in the current wave of artificial intelligence (AI) development.

Altman studied computer science at Stanford University but dropped out after two years. He co-founded Loopt, a location-based social networking mobile app, which raised over $30 million in venture capital. He joined the startup accelerator Y Combinator in 2011 and served as its president from 2014 to 2019. He also served as chairman of the clean energy companies Helion Energy and Oklo (until April 2025). As of May 2025, his net worth is estimated at $1.5 billion.

Altman was born in Chicago, Illinois, to a Jewish family and raised in St. Louis, Missouri. His mother was a dermatologist and his father a real estate broker. He is the eldest of four children. He received his first computer—an Apple Macintosh—at the age of eight and began programming and disassembling hardware. He attended the private John Burroughs School in Ladue, Missouri. He enrolled at Stanford in 2005 to study computer science but dropped out after two years.

In 2005, at the age of 19, Altman co-founded Loopt and served as CEO. The app raised over $30 million from investors including Xfund, NEA, Sequoia Capital, and Y Combinator. In March 2012, due to limited user growth, Loopt was acquired by Green Dot Corporation for $43.4 million.

Altman became a partner at Y Combinator in 2011. In February 2014, YC founder Paul Graham named him president. That year, Altman said the total valuation of YC-backed startups had exceeded $65 billion, including companies such as Airbnb, Dropbox, Zenefits, and Stripe. He aimed to fund 1,000 startups per year and expressed particular interest in hard tech.

In April 2012, Altman co-founded the venture capital firm Hydrazine Capital with his brother Jack Altman, focusing on early-stage tech investments.

In October 2015, he announced the formation of YC Continuity, a $700 million fund for later-stage YC companies. The same month, he donated $10 million to launch Y Combinator Research, a nonprofit research lab.

In December 2015, Altman co-founded OpenAI with Elon Musk, Jessica Livingston, Peter Thiel, and others to ensure that artificial general intelligence (AGI) benefits humanity. The project was initially backed by $1 billion in pledges from Microsoft, AWS, and others.

In September 2016, he was appointed president of YC Group. In March 2019, Altman stepped down to focus on OpenAI, becoming its CEO. He officially left Y Combinator in early 2020.

In 2019, Altman co-founded Tools for Humanity, a company that developed iris-scanning devices for biometric identification. Participants receive a cryptocurrency called Worldcoin. The project has been trialed in countries like Kenya but faced regulatory scrutiny over privacy and fraud concerns from authorities in France, the UK, South Korea, and others.

Altman has also invested in other companies, including Humane (AI wearables), Retro Biosciences (longevity), Cruise (acquired by GM), and Helion Energy (nuclear fusion).

As CEO of OpenAI, Altman helped raise $1 billion from Microsoft in 2019. In May 2023, he testified before the U.S. Senate on AI regulation and then traveled to 22 countries meeting with world leaders to discuss AI governance.

On November 17, 2023, the OpenAI board abruptly removed Altman and co-founder Greg Brockman, citing concerns about Altman’s lack of candor. Brockman resigned. The move triggered a major backlash—over 700 employees threatened to quit and join Microsoft. The board eventually resigned, and Altman and Brockman were reinstated on November 22.

In March 2024, law firm WilmerHale completed an investigation and Altman rejoined the board.

In May 2024, controversy emerged over OpenAI's severance agreements, which reportedly included non-disparagement clauses. Altman was accused of misrepresenting his knowledge of these clauses. In the same month, former board member Helen Toner stated that the reasons for Altman’s 2023 ousting included concealing the release of ChatGPT, failing to disclose his ownership in OpenAI’s venture fund, allegations of psychological manipulation from employees, and chaotic leadership during his Loopt days.

In 2014, Altman briefly served as interim CEO of Reddit for eight days and helped bring back co-founder Steve Huffman as CEO. He served on Reddit’s board until 2022 and was one of its major investors. By Reddit’s 2024 IPO, Altman was the company’s third-largest shareholder, holding roughly 9%.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, he funded Project Covalence to streamline clinical trials. During the 2023 Silicon Valley Bank collapse, he helped provide emergency funding to startups.

He has also chaired Helion Energy and Oklo Inc., and invested in Airbnb, Stripe, Retro Biosciences, and solar energy startup Exowatt.

In 2021, Altman co-founded a SPAC, AltC Acquisition Corp., with banker Michael Klein and served as CEO. In May 2024, Oklo merged with AltC and went public, with Altman as chairman until stepping down in April 2025 to avoid conflicts of interest.
